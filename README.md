# Beyond the Code: 4 Keys from a Senior Data Analyst for Generating Insights That Move the Needle

I’ve been working with data to inform business decisions for 15 years. During that time, I’ve learned that the difference between an average analyst and a valuable one isn’t in the tech stack, it’s in how they think.

# 1. Context
Generative Artificial Intelligence has made the importance of context even more evident.

An analyst who lacks context regarding the business or the metrics they generate is unable to produce meaningful insights or recommendations.

At WS Audiology, I learned that it is essential to understand the business, its value chain, and how each number in the report is derived; without this foundation, the data remains noise.

A senior analyst has a technical foundation for analysis but has also developed a business perspective. These two skills allow them to bridge both worlds and generate insights.

# 2. Methodology
Methodology is an analyst’s most important asset: their methodology must have been tested in various scenarios and must be bulletproof.

A methodology allows you to address all business problems in a systematic way and consistently achieve results.

Statistical analysis methodologies such as PPDAC (Problem - Plan - Data - Analysis - Communication) already exist. In most cases, it is not necessary to invent something new; simply leverage what others have already done.

# 3. Problem-solving mindset
I believe the ability to identify the root cause of a problem and solve it is more valuable than hard skills in SQL and Python. It's a rare skill, and without it, it's impossible to leverage technology to tackle organizational challenges.

# 4. Intellectual curiosity
Being an expert in analysis (knowing business, statistics, SQL, Python, AI, and more) is the bare minimum for any analyst, but having knowledge across different subjects allows you to connect the dots across other areas to generate valuable insights.

The analyst is the Sherlock Holmes of today. The more they know about history, physics, chemistry, and various sectors such as fintech, marketing, and logistics, the more they can add value with innovative, high-impact solutions.
